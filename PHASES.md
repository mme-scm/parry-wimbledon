# Phases
Run in order and never skip a phase. After each phase: update STATUS.md, commit, push.

## Phase 0: find the corpus
Run corpus-scout. Then check its claims yourself: for every USE source, open at least one downloaded file and confirm the claimed fields exist.
Choose the match and scope. Prefer, in this order:
1. a match with spoken commentary from more than one broadcaster;
2. a match with timing data;
3. the richest single-broadcaster match.
Record the choice and your reasons in STATUS.md, and replace the Match and Corpus scope lines in CLAUDE.md.
If no spoken-commentary source is usable, continue with the best material available, and state prominently in STATUS.md, and later in the paper, what that limits.

## Phase 1: tools and corpus
Run homer-tools and corpus-builder in parallel. Then verify their work yourself instead of trusting their summaries:
(1) Scan 25 random Homeric lines by your own reasoning and compare with scan.py. Record each disagreement and who is right.
(2) Check 5 well-known formulae and 5 random n-grams from the index with concordance.py against homer/lines.tsv.
(3) Read 3 random minutes of transcript and judge the claimed error rate.
(4) Check 20 rally timings against their source: spectrogram PNGs where there is audio, annotation records otherwise.
Send failures back to the responsible agent until the results are acceptable. Record the validation figures in STATUS.md.

## Phase 2: analyses
Run formula-analyst and metre-analyst in parallel. Confirm from git log that analysis/metre/plan.md was committed before any metre test ran.
Run critic on analysis/ (phase "analysis"). For each critical or major issue, either have the responsible agent fix it or record why it stands in analysis/limitations.md. Run critic once more.
Write a summary of at most 10 sentences in STATUS.md: numbers, CIs, and the three main limitations.

## Phase 3: brief
Write composition/brief.md containing:
(1) Match facts verified from corpus/ or cited official statistics, with timestamps.
(2) A plan for about 50 lines: proem; entry and arming as a typical scene; the duel set by set, with 2–3 decisive points expanded; an aristeia; at least 2 extended similes, each modeled on a cited Homeric simile; the ending.
(3) The ten most frequent commentary formulae or systems, each paired with a Homeric functional equivalent found via the concordance (with citations), to be used where the commentators used theirs.
(4) Each player gets at least 3 name-epithet formulae of different metrical shapes, each modeled on a cited Homeric formula of that shape.

## Phase 4: composition
Loop for at most 5 rounds:
(1) composer writes v{N}.
(2) In parallel: scansion-verifier (give it only v{N}.txt), provenance-verifier, philologist.
(3) Merge their verdicts into review/round_{N}.md. A line passes only if all three pass it.
(4) composer revises the failing lines. QUERY lines go to FOR_HUMAN.md.
Stop when every line is PASS or QUERY, or after round 5.
Then run critic (phase "poem"). composer does one revision for critical and major issues, followed by one verifier round on the changed lines.
Freeze the result as composition/final/poem.txt and poem.jsonl. Record in STATUS.md: line count, rounds, the poem's formulaic density and method, and the % of lines containing an ATTESTED-EXACT formula.

## Phase 5: translation
Run translator. Then check:
(1) Line counts match.
(2) Each repeated Greek formula has identical English at every occurrence; list them.
(3) Scan 10 English lines.
Fix any failures through translator. Write composition/final/facing.md as a two-column table, Greek left and English right.

## Phase 6: paper
Write paper/paper.md, about 4,000 words, for specialists. Cover: the question; corpus sources and their limits; methods; results, with figures generated by scripts in paper/figures/; the composition experiment as a reverse test of thrift and extension; where the commentary–Homer analogy fails; limitations; appendices (poem, translation, scansion).
Cite only works verified by fetching a catalogue or publisher record. Run critic on the paper and fix critical and major issues.

## Phase 7: weaving and stitching
Add a section to the paper covering: ῥαψῳδός and ῥάπτω; ὑφαίνω and *webʰ-; Pindaric and Vedic examples; the comparative scholarship.
Quote primary passages in the original with citations, verified against fetched texts. Back each etymology with a dictionary entry actually consulted (Beekes, Chantraine, LIV), or mark it [unverified].
Connect the section to the poem's composition records. Run critic on the section and fix critical and major issues.

## Finish
Write README.md: what was done, the corpus sources, where each deliverable is, the headline results, and a summary of FOR_HUMAN.md. Mark every phase DONE in STATUS.md. Commit and push.

## Phase 2b (added 2026-10-07 at the human's request, after Phase 2 closed): the tradition across broadcasts
Exploratory relative to the pre-registered Phase 2; its own plan (analysis/parry/plan.md) is committed before any test runs.
1. Shared stock versus idiolect: pairwise formula sharing between the 20 TV broadcasts, by situational slot and by commentator-hint cluster, against a unigram-preserving shuffle null and the written/press baselines; the genre-wide core as a function of the number of broadcasts attesting a formula.
2. Calibration of the automatic formula measure against Parry's definition: two blind coders mark formula spans in a fixed 300-utterance sample; inter-coder agreement; precision and recall of the automatic inventory (n ≥ 2, n ≥ 3, systems) against the hand-coded spans.
3. Thrift and extension per situational slot across the pool, with the pooled N.
Run critic on analysis/parry; fix critical and major issues or record them in analysis/limitations.md; fold the results into the paper as an exploratory section.
