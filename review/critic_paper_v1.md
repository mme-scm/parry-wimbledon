## 2. Critical

**C1. §7.3 misreads the Rigveda, against the paper's own evidence file, and builds the section's comparative conclusion on it.**
Where: §7.3 "The Rigveda keeps the same two figures apart ...: the sacrifice is woven with threads (tantu) and weft (otu), which in 6.9.2–3 the father knows and the son does not ...; the hymn is fashioned (takṣ-) like a chariot"; the closing "Greek splits the figures as Vedic does"; §7.4 "later Greek gave the crafts to two kinds of poet"; §7.5 "what no Homeric singer says of himself".
(a) The paper itself prints `RV_1,061.08a asmā id u gnāś cid devapatnīr indrāyārkam ahihatya ūvuḥ` in the quotation block: ūvuḥ is the 3 pl. perfect of √ve/vā 'weave' (wisdomlib's grammatical annotation on the verse gives root *ve* 'weave'; Wilson renders "addressed their hymns", Griffith and Jamison–Brereton "wove" [the last two unverified here]), and its object is arkam, the hymn. `weaving_primary.md` §6 says exactly this in its note ("ūvuḥ 'they wove' the arka — in fact a weaving verb ... worth keeping"). The same file quotes RV 10.53.6c "anulbaṇaṃ vayata joguvām apo" (weave without knots the singers' work) and RV 2.3.6. So the Rigveda does weave the song, and the prose asserts the opposite of the line it quotes. Mayrhofer's gloss of Avestan vaf- as „*weben" der Lieder, which the paper quotes in §7.2, is the same figure; §7.3's "Avestan vaf- reaches song without a figure" dismisses the dictionary's own reading. The weaving of song is the standard Indo-European comparandum in exactly the works the paper lists as "cited by verified records only, arguments unread" (Durante 1960; Schmitt 1967; West 2007 ch. 1); a specialist reader will know this before reaching the sentence.
(b) "which in 6.9.2–3 the father knows and the son does not" is not in the text. 6.9.2: the speaker ("I") does not know warp or weft, and asks "whose son could speak here the things to be spoken, beyond his father below" (kasya svit putra iha vaktvāni paro vadāty avareṇa pitrā). 6.9.3: "he" (sa) knows warp and weft and speaks in due order, "he who perceives this, the guardian of the immortal" — Agni Vaiśvānara, the hymn's deity (Wilson; Sāyaṇa takes the "father below" as Agni and the speaker-to-be as the sun). Nothing says the father knows and the son does not; the paper's gloss reverses the question.
Consequence: the comparative half of §7 (Greek keeps song for ῥάπτω/τέκτων and cloth for ὑφαίνω "as Vedic does") is unsupported and in part false; what survives is Greek-internal (Homer does not weave song; Pindar and Bacchylides do) and the truthful comparative statement is the reverse: Vedic (and Avestan) weave songs where Homer does not, which Pindar then does.
Settle: read RV 1.61.8, 10.53.6 and 2.3.6 in any translation (Wilson at wisdomlib.org doc829625; Griffith; Jamison–Brereton) and Mayrhofer II 506 as already quoted in `weaving_primary.md` §5.4; rewrite §7.3's Vedic paragraph as "the Rigveda weaves both the sacrifice (10.130, 6.9) and the hymn (1.61.8, 10.53.6), and also fashions it (takṣ-, 1.130.6, 1.61.4); Homer, by contrast, never weaves a song"; delete the 6.9 gloss or replace it with what the verse says; drop "Greek splits the figures as Vedic does". Rank critical because the section was commissioned for specialists in Indo-European studies and the error is against the paper's own fetched text.

## 3. Major

**M1. §8 compares coverages at unequal identification size and reads the gap as sharing.** "repeats identified on other matches cover 55.0% of the final's tokens against 20.3% from the other half of the same match ...: a shared diction exceeding any one commentator's self-repetition, as a tradition should". The pool identification set is about 100,000 tokens, the half-match about 4,900; coverage rises steeply with identification size (Phase 2 D5(iii): the pool measured against itself at pool size gives 69.8%, `density_medium.csv`; in-sample 2019 with 9.8k tokens gives 44.2%). Nothing in the results separates size from sharing in this contrast, and the paper's own matched-size design (Table 5: TV 59.7 vs Cornell 44.6 vs press 46.6 at 100k) is the one that supports a sharing claim. Settle: identify on a 4,900-token subsample of the pool (R = 200) and measure on the 2019 final; if that figure is near 20.3, the sentence must go; otherwise report it. Until then, replace the 55.0-vs-20.3 contrast with the Table 5 contrast.

**M2. Three sentences overclaim a null or a selected-set ratio, beyond `limitations.md` 4, 12, 16 and `critic_parry_v2` N1.** (i) §6 last sentence: "The commentary has the stock and none of the slot-bound choice" (C4/C5 are nulls with MDE θ 0.30–0.42, ρ 0.17–0.22; H4b is "inconclusive", p 0.0055–0.123; §9.3, §9.9). (ii) §7.5: "without a trace of the slot-bound choice that would make the repetition a loom" (same). (iii) §7.5: "the commentators of §4 ... repeat strings at eight times the rate of other registers (§5)" — the median rate ratio 8.4 is a property of the TV-only strings, a selected set covering about a tenth of the tokens, as §5 and §9.7 say; the commentators do not repeat "strings" at eight times anyone's rate. §8's phrasing ("the strings that make the difference recur in commentary at eight times their rate elsewhere") is the correct one. Settle: the paper's own §9 items 3, 7 and 9; rewrite (i) and (ii) as "no slot-bound choice was detected (MDE ...)" and (iii) as in §8.

**M3. §6 gives a false reason for a true number.** "the verbatim lines carry part of it (81.9% → 74.7%) but none of the stricter figure (53.8% → 54.0% for content strings attested twice), since a line Homer uses once contributes nothing there." Under the min2 definition a once-used verbatim line contributes every sub-n-gram attested twice elsewhere; from the paper's own figures the 19 verbatim lines (137 tokens) have min2 coverage (0.538 × 485 − 0.540 × 348) / 137 = 53%, not nothing. The correct statement is that the verbatim lines are no more covered than the composed lines under the stricter definition. Settle: per-line n2_base_min2 coverage of the 19 verbatim lines from `review/poem_density.py`; expect about 53%.

**M4. §7.3 "Homer's τέκτων (12 lines) is always a worker in wood or horn, never a maker of words (`--loose "επεων τεκτ"`: 0)".** The noun count is right, but the query is a straw test and the claim is false for the family: Od. 14.131 αἶψά κε καὶ σύ, γεραιέ, ἔπος παρατεκτήναιο ("you too would quickly fashion a tale") applies the τεκτ- verb to ἔπος, and Il. 10.19 μῆτιν ἀμύμονα τεκτήναιτο applies it to μῆτις, the same object ὑφαίνω takes; Il. 14.54 παρατεκτήναιτο is Zeus "re-fashioning" events. Together with Pindar's ἐπέων τέκτονες (P.3.113, which the paper leaves unfetched) this is the Greek side of Vedic takṣ- vācam and belongs in the section, not against it. Settle: `python homer/concordance.py --regex "τεκτήν|τεκταίν"` (4 lines); reword to "the noun τέκτων is always a craftsman in wood or horn; the verb τεκταίνομαι takes μῆτιν (Il. 10.19) and, once, ἔπος (Od. 14.131)".

**M5. §7.5 presents the stitching/weaving mapping as a count, not a metaphor.** (i) "The 297 source entries ... are the stitched units: 151 ..., 115 modified, 31 coined names ..., and 19 whole lines sewn in entire" — the 19 are inside the 151 (`poem_stats.json` verbatim_homeric_lines.all_have_whole_line_exact_source), so the list double-counts. (ii) "By the count, stitching outnumbers weaving" compares 297 entries with 75 modifications, but 115 of the 297 entries are themselves modified pieces; entries and operations are not commensurable units, and by the paper's own mapping 39% of the stitched pieces were "woven". (iii) "Nine pieces in ten are taken and one in ten, the names, made: Kent's læs and wæf name that proportion, which is what Pindar's ῥαπτῶν ἐπέων says of the Homeridae" — neither Elene 1237 nor N.2.2 says anything about a proportion; this is decoration. (iv) "the Homeric repeats of §7.2 already show every kind: ... substitution in a kept frame (34; τὼ περὶ νίκης for Od. 1.1's ὃς μάλα πολλά)" — the example is from the poem, not from Homer; the Homeric block does contain one (Od. 2.93 ἡ δὲ δόλον τόνδ᾽ ἄλλον vs 24.128 ἀλλὰ δόλον τόνδ᾽ ἄλλον), which should be the one cited. The legitimate content of the paragraph is one sentence: the records distinguish pieces taken whole from pieces modified, and Homer's own verb for the plan behind a text is ὑφαίνω μῆτιν. Settle: the numbers are in `poem_stats.json`; cut (ii)–(iii), fix (i) and (iv).

**M6. §6 "pieces that keep their Homeric positions in 63 of 75 modifications" and "substitution ... and inflection ... leave the frame where Homer has it".** Only entries labelled `mobility` are counted as moved; the records show substitutions that move the inserted piece (line 1: "τὼ περὶ is attested only at 1-2 (Il. 16.756): mobility" inside a substitution entry, `poem_stats.json` modifications.substitution.example), and separations (4) by definition change relative position. 63/75 is therefore an upper bound on position-keeping, and the frame/piece distinction should be stated. Settle: count substitution entries whose `homeric_parallel` text contains "mobility" or "not a Homeric position" in `poem.jsonl`; report "frames kept in 63 of 75; the inserted piece keeps an attested slot in N of the 34 substitutions".
